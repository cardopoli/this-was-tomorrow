## Do you have a story?

I'm looking to hear from people who live in or run a community-led housing project, cohousing scheme, Community Land Trust, or cooperative living arrangement anywhere in the UK - or any other way of living together, whether it happened in the past, is happening now, or might only exist so far as an idea.

If that's you - or if you know someone I should speak to - get in touch [here](mailto:info@enricopolicardo.com).

## Who I am

I'm a photographer and artist researching how people adapt and sustain the places they inherit - particularly where neighbourhoods function through collective action, shared ownership and everyday work rather than top-down regeneration.

This phase of work continues a long-term investigation into how communities inherit places shaped by past visions, and how those visions shift, erode or reappear. The title reflects the distance between what was promised and what is lived, while asking: what futures can still be imagined from here?

## How this investigation evolved

This ACE-funded project builds on visual research that began in 2022 - documenting [endangered council estates in London](https://enricopolicardo.com/tagged/thiswastomorrow) and the utopian ideals embedded in their material form. A residency at [ESAD IDEA in Porto](https://enricopolicardo.com/tagged/voltoja) shifted the focus. Rather than documenting loss, the investigation now examines how places actually function in the present - across different models of living and sustaining, collective ownership, adaptation, repair, and resilience.

The central question: what futures are still imaginable from where we are now? [Published editions and earlier iterations](https://enricopolicardo.com/tagged/books) are archived here. Everything remains interconnected.

## Supporters & advisors

This work is supported by Arts Council England through the Developing Your Creative Practice (DYCP) scheme.

I'm working alongside experienced practitioners and organisations: [Open City](https://open-city.org.uk/) (Simon Vickery, Open House Festival Programme Manager); [Thaddeus Zupancic](https://shop.greyscape.com/product/london-estates-modernist-council-housing-1946-1981-by-thaddeus-zupancic/), Slovenian-born writer and photographer documenting London's modernist council housing; and Judith Martin, architectural historian and conservationist, co-author of the original This Was Tomorrow publication.

Their input shapes what I'm looking for on the ground and how I understand what I'm seeing - connecting this phase of work back to earlier investigations while pushing toward different geographies and models.

## The work

This phase looks at places that are functioning in the present, without framing them as models or solutions, but as lived situations shaped through use, negotiation and maintenance.

I'm researching across four broad strands:

- Existing estates and neighbourhoods
- Retrofit, reuse and repair as ongoing practice
- CLTs, co-housing and collective ownership
- Places that continue to function despite underfunding or institutional strain

I work across multiple time layers - looking at housing ideals embedded in existing estates, how places function now (often imperfectly), and possibilities already visible on the ground for different futures.

## How I work

I work slowly and collaboratively: spending time on site, listening, walking, joining local sessions, and speaking with residents and organisers. My presence is non-intrusive. Often it involves observing, understanding context, and building trust before making images.

I don't photograph people as a primary subject. The work focuses on places, interiors, objects, traces of use and spatial relationships. Photographs and sound are kept separate: images don't illustrate voices, and voices don't explain images. The intention is to leave space for interpretation rather than producing explanatory case studies.

Any audio recording is optional and fully consent-based. All images and recordings are used only after explicit consent is given.

## What I'm documenting

- How places are actually used - the everyday infrastructure of communal living
- Details of maintenance, adaptation, repair and care
- Shared spaces and how they function
- Material traces of decision-making and collective work
- The visual language of resilience and negotiation

## What becomes available to you

After a visit, you'll receive professional photographs and any recorded audio. These are yours to use freely - for your website, archives, events, local displays, or however you see fit.

**Timeline:** Images will be shared relatively soon after a visit. However, the final body of work will need curation, redaction and editing - this takes longer. You won't need to wait for the finished project to get materials; I'll share as work progresses.

**Exhibition and publication:** The work will be exhibited in 2026-27, potentially at places I visit and during the Open House Festival. A small photo book is also possible. You'll be credited and involved in how the work is presented.

## About a visit

**Timing:** Once we've touched base, I'll propose specific dates. Everything is negotiable.

**Duration:** I typically spend a few days in a place, sometimes longer. The commitment from you is light - I'm here to observe and be present, not to consume everyone's time.

**Where I stay:** I'd prefer accommodation that allows reflection - independent spaces, local guesthouses, or community spaces rather than chain hotels. If you have suggestions, I'm genuinely interested. I have a small budget to contribute.
